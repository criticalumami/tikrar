Proposals · slip-cast porcelain lanterns

One module, a whole Beirut street

Every lantern is built from one porcelain module: a 60 × 60 mm square with 40 mm walls, 1:75 of a Beirut room. Its four walls take openings from the 1860–1925 houses or from Beirut's modernism; modules stack into houses and towers, and houses and towers line up into a village, all within the proportions of the two restoration manuals.

Rules used throughout: no non-porcelain accessories; for now everything is flush with the walls; every wall passes the porcelain limits (openings ≥ 1.6 mm, bars ≥ 2.5 mm, ≤ 40 % open, 3 mm from corners and edges); every fired piece ≤ 60 mm tall.

Open the Beit Lantern Designer → Village and building editor in 3D: pick openings for every wall, candles and plates per level, roof, clay and glaze, checks, and a CAD technical sheet as PDF.

Summary

Six ideas

01

One module at 1:75

60 × 60 mm is a 4.5 m room bay and 40 mm a 3.0 m wall. One mould family makes every building; only the wall patterns change.

02

Openings from two books

Triple arches, līwān arches, hilālī doors, coupled windows and oculi from Houses of Beirut 1860–1925; ribbons, claustras, fins and portholes from Architectures de Beyrouth.

03

Houses and towers on one street

Modules stack up to eight high and sit three across and three deep. Central-hall houses keep a square plan, one to three levels and a symmetric front; modernist blocks may rise.

04

Porcelain-safe by construction

Every opening, bar and wall is checked as you design. Every one of the 4,096 element combinations and 134 whole-wall designs passes.

05

Light per level

Each level can sit on its own plate and carry its own tealights; plates and roofs are vented. The roof covers the whole house in one piece, or each module.

+

Drawings for the workshop

Each building exports a CAD technical sheet: general arrangement, elevations, plan, section, stacking detail and every wall type dimensioned, with mould sizes.

Houses of Beirut 1860–1925

Pick your openings

The printed piece is one porcelain module: a 60 × 60 mm square with 40 mm walls, 3 mm thick. Base, modules and roof are separate pieces: modules stack on porcelain lips (as many levels as you like) or line up side by side, under a slanted or modern flat roof. For now everything is flush with the walls: openings only, no mouldings, balconies or overhangs. Each wall takes typical openings, three elements side by side or one whole-wall design, or a random draw.

Beirut 1945–1970

Modernist patterns and elements

The same 60 × 40 mm wall, read through Beirut's modernism: ribbon windows, claustra screens, vertical brise-soleil slots, egg-crate grids, portholes and buildings lifted on pilotis. All cut flush through the porcelain wall, within the same casting limits.

Whole-wall patterns

From Architectures de Beyrouth 1925–1970, Manuel de restauration (Beirut Heritage Initiative, 2022): typologies PDF pp. 16–20, cantilevers p. 38, brise-soleil and claustras pp. 52–53, windows pp. 78–93. Landmarks

Iconic modernist Beirut, as lanterns

Proposed landmark lanterns, after studying photographs of each building. Each keeps the building's signature elements and its proportions (storey counts, height-to-width ratios, bay rhythm, measured from photos to about ±10 %) and is designed to survive casting and firing.

All six are drawn at the same scale, 1 unit = 1 mm fired, so their sizes compare directly. A village house is 180 mm wide. Each landmark gets its own scale (1:200–1:400): at the village's 1:75 a 14-storey tower would be 0.6 m.

Porcelain-safe rules. Porcelain softens at top temperature, so tall thin walls lean and lattice-like walls crack or slump. Every landmark follows four rules:
  • Fired sections ≤ 60 mm, each fired on its own, then stacked dry on 3 mm porcelain locating lips, with no glue. The tealight sits in the base section.
  • Each wall ≤ 40 % open. The rest of a facade pattern (fins, slab edges, deep cells) is relief: raised 0.8–1 mm or recessed 1 mm. The card's figure is computed from the drawing.
  • 3 mm walls for anything over one section, and fins and bars ≥ 2.5 mm wherever they run long.
  • ≤ 40 pins per insert set, as in the studio.

Step by step

The making process

Designer sizes are fired millimetres. Moulds and prints are master size, fired ÷ (1 − shrink): ×1.163 for porcelain at 14 %; the technical sheet states the factor for the chosen clay. Improvements are marked ▶.

Editor→Mold design→Resin case molds→Plaster mold→Slip cast→Fettle + extras→Fire→Lantern

    1. Relief back on frames, cornices, string courses (switch FLUSH off)
