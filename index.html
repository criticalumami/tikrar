Proposals · slip-cast porcelain lantern houses

Making the lanterns read as Beirut

How the Beit Lantern project can take the proportions, composition and cast ornament of Beirut houses (1860–1970), keep everything modular, and make every new element from porcelain alone.

Rule used throughout: no non-porcelain accessories. Every new element is cast from the same slip as the modules. The wood roofs and slabs already in the editor stay as they are.

Open Beit Lantern Studio → A modular 3D editor that applies these proposals, with mold counts and feature checks, including the porcelain-safe rules.

Summary

Five ideas that matter most

01

A 1:75 scale

The app's numbers already sit on it: 60 mm is a 4.5 m room bay, a 44 mm floor is a 3.30 m ceiling, and the 14 mm low balcony is a 1.05 m guard-rail. Making it explicit lets every opening be checked.

02

Compose houses, not modules

A Beirut front has one central bay flanked by plainer side rooms, about three modules wide. Today almost every module carries its own triple arch.

03

Openwork in porcelain

Balustrades and claustra screens are dense patterns of openings, so the existing resin insert sets can make them. Lit from inside, they glow.

04

Pull the pins early

Porcelain shrinks onto rigid resin pins as it stiffens and cracks at the corners of openings. Draft the pins 1–2° and pull the sets as soon as the drained cast loses its shine.

05

Era presets

Late Ottoman and Modern each have clear rules for openings, symmetry, balconies and roofs: a village with variety that still holds together.

+

Translucent panes

A 0.6–1 mm porcelain sheet behind some openings glows like the frosted mhajjar glass of Beirut doors, and hides the tealight.

Houses of Beirut 1860–1925

Pick your openings

The printed piece is one porcelain module: a 60 × 60 mm square with 40 mm walls, 3 mm thick. Base, modules and roof are separate pieces: modules stack on porcelain lips or line up side by side, under a slanted or modern flat roof. Each wall takes typical openings, either three elements side by side or one whole-wall design.

Beirut 1945–1970

Modernist patterns and elements

The same 60 × 40 mm wall, read through Beirut's modernism: ribbon windows under thin sun-shade slabs, claustra screens, vertical brise-soleil fins, egg-crate grids, portholes and buildings lifted on pilotis. All in porcelain, all within the same casting limits.

Landmarks

Iconic modernist Beirut, as lanterns

Proposed landmark lanterns, after studying photographs of each building. Each keeps the building's signature elements and its proportions (storey counts, height-to-width ratios, bay rhythm, measured from photos to about ±10 %) and is designed to survive casting and firing.

All six are drawn at the same scale, 1 unit = 1 mm fired, so their sizes compare directly. A village house is 180 mm wide. Each landmark gets its own scale (1:200–1:400): at the village's 1:75 a 14-storey tower would be 0.6 m.

Porcelain-safe rules. Porcelain softens at top temperature, so tall thin walls lean and lattice-like walls crack or slump. Every landmark follows four rules:
  • Fired sections ≤ 60 mm, each fired on its own, then stacked dry on 3 mm porcelain locating lips, with no glue. The tealight sits in the base section.
  • Each wall ≤ 40 % open. The rest of a facade pattern (fins, slab edges, deep cells) is relief: raised 0.8–1 mm or recessed 1 mm. The card's figure is computed from the drawing.
  • 3 mm walls for anything over one section, and fins and bars ≥ 2.5 mm wherever they run long.
  • ≤ 40 pins per insert set, as in the studio.

Step by step

The making process

    For the app

    Roadmap

    Changes to the editor, Mold making and 3D print tabs, in priority order. All keep the existing rules: one file, global heights, the 30 mm grid, and a green fit check.

    Produceable P1

    1. STL export per part with manifold-3d, so parts can leave the app
    2. Pin draft 1–2° on pins and ports, with a fit-check line
    3. Minimum-feature check opening, porcelain bar and resin pin sizes
    4. Clips and strap grooves on case-mold parts

    Lebanese composition P2

    1. New opening types segmental arch, wide segmental bay, stepped lintel, oculus, ribbon, tall Qotrani; single openings, no bars
    2. Era presets defaults, allowed openings and symmetry rule per era
    3. "Beirut house" preset three modules, central bay, joined hipped roof
    4. Generator: main facade and axis shared sill lines, rich opening on the axis only
    5. 1:75 readout and soft warnings on off-proportion openings
    6. House from photo reads with this vocabulary

    Porcelain elements P3

    1. Balustrade and claustra patterns as opening arrays on insert sets
    2. Relief layer frames, cornice, sill line, panels, grooves, cartouche, with a draft check
    3. Pane flag on openings night view glows, a pane mold per size
    4. Accessory molds balcony and consoles, kiosk, columns, with firing props

    Production helpers P4

    1. Mold count panel "this village needs 5 module molds"
    2. Facade swapping one frame mold, interchangeable facades
    3. Batch log measured shrink feeds back into the app
    4. Printable process sheet per mold
