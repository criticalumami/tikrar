# 05: Iconic modernist Beirut, as porcelain-safe landmark lanterns

Proposed landmark lanterns, after studying photographs of each building. Each one keeps the building's signature elements and its proportions: storey count, height-to-width ratio and bay rhythm. Ratios were measured from photographs and are good to about ±10 %. Each lantern is also designed to survive casting and firing. The website's "Beirut modernism" section draws all six at one common scale (1 unit = 1 mm fired), with section joints dashed and the open share of each wall computed from the drawing.

## Why the first drafts would have failed

Porcelain softens at top temperature, so tall thin walls lean and lattice-like walls crack when the pins come out or slump in the kiln. The first drafts passed the studio's checks (openings and bars ≥ 1.6 mm, ≤ 40 pins per set) but not these limits:

| Draft | Problem |
|---|---|
| EDL | 51 % of the wall open, with 1.7 mm fins |
| Gefinor | 53 % open, with long 1.6 mm fins |
| Saroulla | 73 % open: the facade was almost all hole |
| Holiday Inn, EDL, Gefinor, Saroulla | 200–230 mm tall, cast and fired in one piece or as bands joined wet, which crack at the joins as they shrink |

## Porcelain-safe rules (all six follow them)

1. **Fired sections of 60 mm or less.** Each section is fired on its own, then stacked dry on 3 mm porcelain locating lips, with no glue. The tealight sits in the base section.
2. **Each wall at most 40 % open.** The rest of a facade pattern (fins, slab edges, deep cells, loggias) becomes relief: raised 0.8–1 mm or recessed 1 mm. Through-openings are smaller windows inside that relief, which is also how the real buildings work.
3. **3 mm walls** for anything taller than one section. **Fins and bars of at least 2.5 mm** wherever they run long.
4. **At most 40 pins per insert set**, as in the studio. One printed comb is reused for every identical section.

## The six lanterns

| Lantern | Scale, fired size | Sections | Max open per wall |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Egg | 1:330, 143 × 75 × 99 mm | four 10 mm frame trays + a 58 mm shell | 23 % |
| Électricité du Liban | 1:300, 220 × 48 × 198 mm | slab 55 / 47 / 47 / 39 mm + service tower 4 × 50 mm | 34 % |
| Gefinor Center | 1:400, 185 × 70 × 196 mm | podium 25 + tower 3 × 55 + lower block 50 mm | 39 % |
| Saroulla building | 1:200, 150 × 75 × 217 mm | cinema base 25 + 4 × 48 mm (3 floors each) | 34 % |
| Holiday Inn | 1:400, 150 × 50 × 230 mm | podium 22 + slab 54 / 54 / 47 / 47 mm + service tower 4 × 52 mm | 37 % |
| National Museum | 1:250, 232 × 120 × 67 mm | plinth 8 + wings 47 + portico 59 mm; columns 40 mm, cast separately | 23 % |

### 1. The Egg (Beirut City Center, from 1965, Joseph Philippe Karam)
An unfinished concrete shell cinema for about 1,000 seats.

- **Keep:**
  - the long rounded capsule with its flat-ish crown
  - the band of rectangular openings along its flank
  - the four-level open frame of slabs and square columns below
- **Proportions:** shell length ≈ 2.3 × shell height. The frame ≈ 0.7 × shell height.
- **Make:**
  - The shell is drain-cast 3 mm thick as an upside-down bowl in a one-piece mould. Its seven flank slots (6 × 8 mm) are cut at leather-hard through a printed guide.
  - The scale is reduced to 1:330 so the shell stays at 58 mm.
  - One tray mould is cast four times. Each tray has a closed core wall with small windows (23 % open), which carries the shell. Slabs and 3.5 mm columns wrap the core as the open frame.

### 2. Électricité du Liban (Mar Mikhaël, competition 1965, built c. 1967–72, CETA: Jacques Aractingi, Pierre Neema, engineer Joseph Nassar)
- **Keep:**
  - the four portal legs, read as strong verticals
  - the sun-breaker grid of 3 × 10 cells per floor
  - the open, lit ground floor
  - the slim, taller service tower
- **Proportions:** long-face height ≈ 0.9 × length. The service tower ≈ 1/8 of the length.
- **Make:**
  - Each cell is a 1 mm recess, and only a 3 × 6 mm window in it goes through, with 2.7 mm fins between windows. One 30-pin set per floor.
  - The portal legs are 6 mm raised relief up the face and solid 6 mm legs at the ground.
  - The slab is cut into four sections at floor lines. The service tower is a separate stack.

### 3. Gefinor Center (Clemenceau, c. 1969–74, Victor Gruen Associates with Assem Salam; structure Walid Jabri)
- **Keep:**
  - the tower of tight vertical fins
  - the blank concrete end walls
  - the lower block of white horizontal bands
  - the podium of piers and shop windows
- **Proportions:** tower height ≈ 2.2 × width. The lower block ≈ 0.55 × its width.
- **Make:**
  - Through-slots are 2 × 16 mm at a 5.4 mm pitch (3.4 mm fins), broken every two floors. A raised 0.8 mm rib between slots restores the dense fin rhythm. 33 pins per tower section.
  - The lower block's windows are 8 × 4 mm under raised white bands, in two sets.

### 4. Saroulla building (Hamra Street, 1961, Karol Schayer)
Offices, shops and the Saroulla cinema, which reopened in 2005 as Al Madina theatre.

- **Keep:**
  - the loggia grid, eight per floor
  - the projecting floor slabs
  - the thin entrance canopy on two round columns
  - the lit cinema base
- **Proportions:** height ≈ 1.45 × width. The ground floor ≈ 1.5 × a typical floor.
- **Make:**
  - Each loggia is a 15.7 × 13.5 mm recess with a 10 × 9 mm window at the back. 24 pins per section.
  - The slab edges are 2.4 mm raised bands.
  - The canopy rests on two B4 porcelain columns, so it needs no prop.

### 5. Holiday Inn (Minet el-Hosn, 1971–74, André Wogenscky with Maurice Hindié)
- **Keep:**
  - the blank, windowless service tower, taller than the slab
  - the dense deep-set window grid, 13 per floor
  - the lit crown floor
  - the podium, wider than the tower
- **Proportions:** slab height ≈ 1.8 × width. The service tower ≈ 1/6 of the width.
- **Make:**
  - Windows are 4.4 × 4.5 mm in 1 mm recesses, with 3.7 mm bars.
  - Pin sets cover up to three floors (39 pins), all from one printed comb.
  - Sections keep the 50 mm-deep slab from leaning in the kiln.

### 6. National Museum of Beirut (Museum crossing, built 1930–37, inaugurated 1942, Antoine Nahas and Pierre Leprince-Ringuet)
Egyptian Revival, not modernist: the landmark from before modernism.

- **Keep:**
  - four lotus-capital columns between two pylons
  - the deep cornice and raised attic block
  - the wings with tall slit windows in threes, in recessed panels
  - the broad stepped plinth
- **Proportions:** the portico is ≈ 0.8 × as tall as it is wide and ≈ 1.35 × the wing height. Each wing is ≈ 1/3 of the front.
- **Make:**
  - Three modules (wing, portico, wing) on a plinth.
  - The attic is trimmed so the portico stays at 59 mm.
  - Four B4 columns, cast solid in a two-part mould with the lotus flare, stand in front of a lit door wall.

## What the studio would need (later, not built)

- A **landmark mode**: one building per piece at its own scale, outside the 60 mm module grid.
- A **section generator**: split a tower at floor lines into sections of 60 mm or less, with locating lips and one reusable pin comb per section type.
- The four porcelain-safe checks (open area per wall ≤ 40 %, section height ≤ 60 mm, 3 mm walls where sections stack, long fins ≥ 2.5 mm) are **now in the studio** for village houses. Landmarks would reuse them.
- A **shell element** for the Egg (a drain-cast form checked for wall thickness), and a **portal / pilotis ground floor** checked for span.
