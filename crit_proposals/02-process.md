# 02 · The process, step by step (with improvements)

The chain: **design → plaster mold design → 3D-print the resin case molds → pour the plaster mold → slip-cast porcelain → finish and fire → assemble and light.**

Sizes: the editor works in **fired** mm. Molds and prints are **master** size = fired ÷ (1 − shrink). At 14 % shrink that is ×1.163.

```
 Editor ──► Mold making ──► 3D print ──► plaster pour ──► slip cast ──► fire ──► lantern
 (fired)     (master)        (resin)      (porous mold)    (porcelain)
```

Improvements are marked **▶**.

---

## Step 1: Design the house (Editor tab)

Build the house from full, half and quarter modules on the 30 mm grid, with one floor height and one low-balcony height for the project. Draw the four facades.

- ▶ Start from an **era preset** and a **3-module main facade** (see 01 §3–4).
- ▶ Keep the module-type count low. Every unique module needs its own mold, so a village of 10 houses should reuse perhaps 4–6 module types. A "mold count" readout in the editor would help (see 04).
- ▶ Use **facade swapping**: one frame mold, several interchangeable facade wall pieces (already open item 5 in the handover). Combined with eras, this gives a lot of variety from few molds.

## Step 2: Design the plaster mold (Mold making tab)

One mold per module type: 4 wall pieces split on the 45° corners, keyed, standing in a lipped base with a resin core that leaves the bottom open; a 45° scrape chamfer at the top; resin insert sets for the openings.

- ▶ Add a **draft check** to the fit check: pins at least 1°, relief at least 3° (see 03).
- ▶ Add a **minimum-feature check**: openings at least 1.5 mm fired, porcelain bars between openings at least 1.5 mm fired, resin pins at least 1.2 mm master. This matters most for balustrade and claustra patterns.
- ▶ Plaster thickness of 25 mm is good. Keep at least 15 mm of plaster behind any pocket so the mold stays absorbent and strong.

## Step 3: Print the resin parts (3D print tab)

Case molds (negatives of each plaster piece), insert sets and the base core, checked against the bed.

- ▶ **STL export** is the gating item (handover item 1). Without it nothing physical can be made from the app. Suggest manifold-3d from jsdelivr, fusing sub-meshes and cutting sockets.
- ▶ Print case-mold facade plates **facade-side up**, so supports never touch the surface that becomes the porcelain facade.
- ▶ Post-cure fully. Under-cured resin leaves a tacky skin that can inhibit the plaster surface.
- ▶ Lightly sand or prime the facade plate if layer lines show; the porcelain will copy them.
- ▶ Add **clips or strap grooves** on case parts (open item 2) so plaster can't push them apart.

## Step 4: Pour the plaster mold pieces

Mold soap as release. Pottery plaster at 70 parts plaster to 100 parts water by weight.

- Sift plaster into the water, slake 2–3 min, mix 3 min without whipping in air.
- ▶ Tap or vibrate the case for 30 s after pouring so bubbles leave the facade face. A bubble on that face becomes a bump on every cast.
- Demold at the plaster's heat peak (about 30–40 min).
- ▶ **Dry the mold fully** before first use: 3–5 days in warm, moving air, or until its weight stops dropping. A wet mold casts slowly and sticks.
- ▶ Mark each piece with its module type and face (pencil on the outside) so sets don't get mixed.

## Step 5: Assemble the mold

Base with core, four walls in the lip (keys locked), insert sets clicked into their pockets, a strap around the walls.

- ▶ Before the first cast, do a **water leak test** only on a spare or retired mold. Water will soak a production mold.
- ▶ Rub a little slip into the 45° joints if a seam line shows on casts; better still, fix the fit in the design.

## Step 6: Slip-cast

1. Use well-mixed deflocculated porcelain slip, at the specific gravity on the slip's datasheet (typically 1.75–1.80). Sieve before use.
2. Pour in one steady stream down a wall, not onto the core. Fill **into the scrape chamfer**.
3. Top up as the level drops.
4. Wait for the wall to build. Calibrate once with a test cast: cut it and measure. For 2.5 mm fired (≈2.9 mm master) expect roughly 10–20 min, depending on the slip and how dry the mold is.
5. Drain fully, inverted at a slight angle, so drips don't form on the facade.
6. ▶ **Pull the window insert sets early**, as soon as the cast surface loses its shine. Porcelain shrinks as it stiffens. Rigid resin pins left in hold it back, which cracks the corners of openings. Pin draft (1–2°) and the chamfered tips make an early pull clean.
7. When the cast is leather-firm, **scrape the top flat** along the chamfer.
8. Strap off, walls out (outward, normal to each face), cast lifted off the core.

## Step 7: Fettle and attach porcelain extras

- Sponge the 45° seam lines and the edges of openings. Fix any blocked opening with a needle tool.
- ▶ Attach porcelain extras (balcony slabs, consoles, window panes, bay windows; see 03) **now, at the same leather-hard state**. Score both surfaces and join with slip (a few drops of vinegar or sodium silicate help). Clean the joint with a soft brush.
- ▶ Stamp or incise the house name, year or initials on the base of the module, like the dates moulded above Beirut entrance doors (p. 36). It is a nice signature detail.

## Step 8: Dry

- Dry slowly and evenly, loosely covered for the first day. Open tubes dry fast at the top edge and can warp.
- ▶ Dry modules **standing on a flat plaster or wood bat** so the bottom edge stays square to the top.

## Step 9: Bisque, then high fire

- Bisque at about 950–1000 °C. Sand or sponge any remaining seam lines (wear a mask, porcelain dust is silica).
- High-fire to the slip's maturing temperature as given on its datasheet.
- ▶ Fire modules **upright on a flat, well-washed shelf** (or on a fired porcelain setter of the same body). That keeps the bottom edge square for stacking.
- ▶ Porcelain softens near maturity. Projecting extras (balconies, bay windows) need a **porcelain prop** of the same body under them during the firing, or must stay short (≤ 6 mm fired) and sit on consoles. See 03.
- ▶ Fire one **translucency test tile**: a strip stepped 0.6, 0.8, 1.0 and 1.2 mm thick. Hold it over a candle to choose the thickness of window panes (03 §3).
- Outside unglazed is the classic white-bisque look. Optional: glaze inside only, which reflects more candlelight out through the openings.

## Step 10: Assemble and light

Stack the modules on the slabs, roof on top, an LED tealight per module on the slab (one candle per module; the editor switches them).

- ▶ Check stacking registration: modules stand on the slab, and the 30 mm grid must line up. Shrinkage varies a little between firings, so measure the first fired batch and correct the `shrink` value in the app.
- ▶ Record actual shrink and wall thickness per batch in a small log (date, slip SG, cast time, shrink %, thickness). After 3–4 batches the app's numbers will be accurate.

## Quick troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Cracks at window corners | cast shrank onto the pins | pull insert sets earlier; add pin draft; round the inside corners of openings ≥ 0.5 mm |
| Opening skinned over | pin too short, or cast too thick | raise pin reach; shorten cast time |
| Seam lines | joints open or plaster chipped | check key fit; chamfer plaster edges 0.3 mm |
| Cast sticks to mold | mold wet or slip too thin | dry the mold; check SG |
| Module leans after firing | uneven drying or a warped shelf | dry on a bat; flat kiln wash |
| Pinholes on facade | bubbles in plaster or slip | vibrate the plaster pour; sieve and rest the slip |
